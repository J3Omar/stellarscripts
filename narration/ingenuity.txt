It weighed about four pounds, carried no science instruments, and held a tiny swatch of fabric from the original Wright Flyer. On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity rose ten feet above Mars.

Ingenuity was a helicopter that rode to Mars beneath the Perseverance rover. NASA wanted to know whether powered flight was even possible in Mars's thin air, which is less than one percent as dense as Earth's. It was meant as a test of up to five flights in thirty days.

That first flight lasted just thirty-nine seconds, and it was fully autonomous, so Earth could not watch it live. Ingenuity had already survived its first freezing night alone, in temperatures that can fall to about minus one hundred thirty degrees Fahrenheit, running on smartphone-industry parts never tested in deep space before.

Then it kept flying. Seventy-two flights over almost three years, scouting terrain for Perseverance and even photographing the parachute that helped the rover land.

On its last flight in January 2024, a rotor was damaged during landing. It can no longer fly, but NASA reported it upright and still in contact.

A test meant to last thirty days proved that flight is possible on another world.
