On September 1, 1979, a spacecraft swept to within about thirteen thousand miles of Saturn and became the first ever to fly by the ringed planet.

That was Pioneer 11, the sister ship of Pioneer 10, launched in April 1973. Its job was to fly past Jupiter, then use Jupiter's gravity, a planet's pull that bends and speeds up a spacecraft's path, to swing across the solar system to Saturn. It flew three times closer to Jupiter than its sister, yet spent less time in Jupiter's radiation belts, because it was moving so fast.

At Saturn, it discovered a narrow new ring, now called the F ring, and a new moon about one hundred twenty-four miles across. It also gave the first proof that Saturn has a magnetic field.

Contact with Pioneer 11 ended in 1995, twenty-two years after launch. It is heading out of the solar system in the opposite direction from Pioneer 10, toward the star Lambda Aquilae, about four million years away. Its project manager said it would travel as a ghost ship in our galaxy.

Before it fell silent, it showed us that Saturn has a magnetic field, a ring nobody knew about, and a moon nobody had seen.
